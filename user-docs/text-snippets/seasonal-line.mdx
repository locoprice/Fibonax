Some sentences belong to a few weeks of the year: Christmas wishes, a closing week, new opening hours. A **seasonal line** puts them into your messages while they apply and leaves them out when they don't. You write the snippet once and it reads right all year.

## Add it to a snippet

1. Open the snippet in the dashboard editor and put the cursor on an empty row, where the wishes should go.
2. In the panel on the left, under **Logic**, open **Seasonal line**.
3. Click **Insert seasonal line**. A blue **Seasonal line** piece appears in the text.

```text
Hello Marco,

here is the quote you asked for.

{seasonal}

Best regards
```

From 8 to 26 December this snippet prints:

```text
Hello Marco,

here is the quote you asked for.

Merry Christmas.

Best regards
```

In July it prints the same message without the wishes, and without a gap where they were.

- Give the line a row of its own. Out of season the row goes, and so does the empty row beside it.
- The preview on the right shows what goes out today. Out of season it says when the next occasion starts.
- The seasonal lines in a snippet are listed under **Seasonal line** in the panel. **Edit** opens Your occasions. **Remove** takes the line out, and **Undo** puts it back.

## Your occasions

One calendar decides what every seasonal line prints. Open it with **Your occasions** in the Seasonal line entry of the editor, or in **Settings > Account > Seasonal line**.

Three occasions are ready, all switched on:

| Occasion | In season | Prints |
|---|---|---|
| Christmas | 8 December to 26 December | Merry Christmas. |
| New Year | 27 December to 6 January | Happy New Year. |
| Easter | 7 days before Easter to 1 day after | Happy Easter. |

Easter moves every year. Fibonax finds its date for you.

- **The switch** beside an occasion turns it off. An occasion that is off never prints.
- **Edit** changes its days and its sentences, then **Save**. **Cancel** leaves it as it was.
- **Add your own** for anything that belongs to some days of the year: a name, the days and the sentence, for example "We are closed until 25 August." You can have up to 10.
- **Remove** takes out one of yours. It asks first.

### Your own wishes

Each occasion has one box per language: English, Italian, Spanish and French. A snippet prints the sentence of its own language.

- A language you leave empty prints the ready words of that language. A snippet in any other language uses English.
- One of your own occasions has no ready words: a language you leave empty uses the first sentence you wrote.
- **Add to the sentence** puts in what your account already knows: **Your name**, **Company name**, **Job title** and **Year**. "Merry Christmas from **Company name**" goes out as "Merry Christmas from LeibTour", with the company of whoever sends it. See [Your details](/text-snippets/dynamic-fields#your-details).
- **Year** is the year of the occasion's own day, so New Year's wishes sent on 28 December already name the year to come.
- The line under the boxes shows what goes out today. When your account is missing a detail the sentence uses, it says so and where to set it. Until then the ready words go out instead, so a wish is never sent half filled.

Every change is saved as you make it. The extension and the phone pick it up within a few minutes.

### When two occasions overlap

One occasion is in season at a time. Your own occasion comes before a ready one. Between two of the same kind, the one whose day is closest to today wins, then the one with the shorter period.

## When you use the snippet

While an occasion is in season, the form shows a row such as **Christmas wishes included**, with **Leave out**. Click it to send this one message without the line. **Put back** brings it back. The next message carries the line again.

- The row shows in the fill window on a page, in the popup and on the phone.
- A snippet with nothing to fill in goes in straight away, wishes included. Delete the sentence in your message when you don't want it that time.
- Dates are read from the clock of the device you send from.
- A snippet in a shared folder prints the occasions of the person who uses it, not of the person who wrote it.
