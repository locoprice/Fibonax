On the Prompts page, click **New prompt**. The editor opens on the right. To change a prompt later, click its card.

Two buttons at the top help you start:

- **Draft from text**: paste text you already have and the AI writes the prompt for you. See [Draft with AI](/draft/overview).
- **See an example**: fills the editor with a finished sample, so you can see what a good prompt looks like.

## Name and task

- **Name**: how the prompt appears in lists and menus. Names are in capital letters: whatever you type or paste turns into capitals.
- **What should the AI do?**: the task, in a sentence or two. Start with an action, like "Write", "Summarise" or "Check". This is the one part every prompt has.

## Add more detail

Four optional parts help the AI do a better job. Each one is a row: click it to add the part, and **Remove** to take it out again.

| Part | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Who the AI is** | A role, like "You are an experienced editor". |
| **Background** | What the AI should know: who it's for, the goal, the facts. |
| **An example** | What a good answer looks like. |
| **Rules** | Length, tone, and what to avoid. |

A part you remove keeps its text in the editor but leaves the prompt. Add it again and the text is back.

The text you copy or insert keeps a heading for each part (Role, Objective, Context, Examples and Constraints), the names AI tools know best.

### Use a Brain as background

In **Background**, choose one of your Brains in the **Brain** box. Everything in that Brain goes into the prompt each time you use it, in the same form the Context button adds to an AI chat, and always as the Brain is that day: change the Brain, and every prompt that uses it is current. Any text you type in Background follows it.

Fibonax puts one line in front of the Brain that names it and asks the AI to base its answer on it and to say when something it needs is missing. The AI knows what the material is about and works from it, rather than guessing.

Choose **None** to take the Brain out. A Brain in the trash, or one that belongs to the person who wrote the prompt, shows as **not available**, and the prompt says so where the Brain would be. Brains are personal, so a teammate who uses your shared prompt gets the prompt with a line in place of your Brain, never its contents.

The [quality score](/prompts/quality-score) counts a Brain as background given.

## Ask me questions first

On by default. The AI asks you clarifying questions before it does the task: one at a time, no more than five, and it asks you to confirm before it gives the final result. Switch it off to skip the questions.

This setting is saved with the prompt.

## Interactive Steps

Off by default. The AI first lists the steps it plans to take, then works one step at a time and waits for your answer before the next.

This setting isn't saved with the prompt. It applies to every prompt you copy, and is kept per browser: the dashboard and the phone page share it in the same browser. The Chrome extension has its own **Interactive Steps** switch in the popup's Prompts tab, for prompts you copy or insert with the extension.

## Temperature

Sets how precise or how creative the AI's answer should be. Pick one of three steps:

| Step | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Precise 0.2** | Facts, data and code. Focused answers that stick to what the AI knows. |
| **Balanced 0.5** | Writing and everyday answers. Clear and natural. |
| **Creative 0.9** | Brainstorming and stories. Varied, original ideas. |

New prompts start on **Balanced**. Click **What is temperature?** under the steps for a short explanation.

Chat apps such as ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini have no temperature setting of their own, so Fibonax adds one short line to the end of the prompt (under **Answer style**) that tells the AI how to answer. You see it in **Preview** and in **Use**, and it travels with the prompt wherever you copy or insert it.

This setting is saved with the prompt. Prompts made before it existed show no step picked and keep their text as it is until you pick one.

## Shortcut, folder and labels

- **Shortcut** (optional): letters, numbers, `-` and `_`. With the shortcut `followup`, typing `"""followup` in any text box inserts the prompt at once. Leave it empty to reach the prompt from the menu only.
- **Folder** and **Labels**: where the prompt is filed, and the labels it shares with your snippets.

## Quality

The **Quality** row shows the prompt's score out of 10 and how many tips are waiting. Click it to see each check and fix most of them in one click. See [Quality score](/prompts/quality-score).

## More options

Click **More options** to set:

- **Strategy**: CoT (think step by step), ToT (compare several ideas), Few-shot (learn from examples), One-shot (learn from one example), RAG (answer from your material) or Agentic (plan and act alone).
- **Intent**: Writing, Coding, Support, SEO, Analysis, Planning, Research or Teaching.
- **Answer format**: Plain, Markdown, JSON or SOP (a step-by-step guide).

While you write, Fibonax may suggest an intent and a strategy here. Click **Use it** to accept them.

## Save and history

Click **Create prompt**, or **Save changes** on a prompt you're editing. On a new prompt, **Preview** shows the full text before you save. Every save is kept: click **History** (the clock button on a narrower screen) to see earlier versions and restore one.
